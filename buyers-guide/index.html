Four questions before we tour

01

What's your monthly number?

Not the most a lender will approve. The payment you sleep well with, taxes, insurance and HOA dues included.

02

What's your timeline?

Moving in three months or in a year? Do you need to sell a home first, or end a lease? Tell me your ideal move-in date.

03

What does the home need?

Bedrooms, an office, a yard, one level, a garage, a view. Tell me the must-haves and the nice-to-haves.

04

What will you trade?

Every buyer trades something. Size for location, a project for a lower price, a longer drive for more land. Knowing your trade saves us weeks.

Lake Oswego specifics

What to check before you fall in love

Lake Oswego homes come with details you won't find in a listing. I check these on every home.

Lake rights

Oswego Lake is private. Access goes with specific addresses, through the Lake Oswego Corporation, for lakefront homes and homes with a lake easement. I check every address.

HOAs

Many neighborhoods and condos have homeowners associations. I pull the dues, rules and reserves before you write an offer.

City or county

Some homes with a Lake Oswego address sit outside city limits. Taxes, permits and services follow the line. I confirm it for every home.

Flood maps

Homes near the lake, canals and rivers carry more flood risk. I pull the map and an insurance quote early.

Tree rules

Lake Oswego protects many trees. Plan a big yard project? I check the city permit rules first.

Older homes

Many homes date to the 1950s through the 1970s. Sewer scopes, roofs and old wiring deserve a close look. I line up the right inspectors.

Offers

Win without overpaying

  • We look at what sold nearby in the last few months, not last year.
  • I call the listing agent to learn what the seller cares about most: price, timing or certainty.
  • We set your top number before we write, so emotion doesn't set it for you.
  • Clean terms win, too. A strong pre-approval and a smart closing date help as much as price.
  • If we lose one, we learn from it and go get the next one.
Money

Costs to plan for

No surprises at the closing table. Here's what to budget for besides your down payment.

Earnest money

A deposit you put down when your offer is accepted. It goes toward your purchase at closing.

Inspection

You pay your inspector. Add a sewer scope or other specialists when the home calls for one.

Appraisal

Your lender orders it, and you usually pay for it.

Closing costs

Lender fees, escrow, title and prepaid taxes and insurance. Your lender gives you a written estimate early.

HOA and lake fees

Some homes come with HOA dues, transfer fees or lake easement dues. I'll pull the numbers for any home you like.

Moving and setup

Movers, utilities and the first trip to the hardware store. Plan for it now.

Good questions

Ask Tam

Do I sign anything before we tour?

Yes. We sign a buyer agreement first. It spells out what I do for you and how I'm paid. I'll walk you through every line.

Do I talk to a lender first or to you?

Talk to me first. I'll connect you with lenders I trust, so you compare real options instead of guessing.

Do I need to be pre-approved?

Before you write an offer, yes. A pre-approval shows sellers you're serious and tells you your real budget.

Can I buy and sell at the same time?

Yes. We plan the timing together so you're not stuck with two homes or none. My Seller's Guide covers the selling side.

Do you help with lakefront homes?

Yes. I hold eXp's Luxury certification, and I'll walk you through lake rights, docks, seawalls and Lake Oswego Corporation rules.

How long does buying take?

Most buyers spend a few weeks to a few months searching. Once your offer is accepted, closing usually takes about a month.
