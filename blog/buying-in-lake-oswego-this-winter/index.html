Buying in Lake Oswego This Winter: Why the Quiet Season Works for You

Fewer buyers, more price cuts and rates on a four-week slide. Here's why winter is a smart time to buy in Lake Oswego and how to make your offer count.

Most people put house hunting on hold once the holiday lights go up. I get it. It's cold, it's dark before 5 and your calendar is packed.

Here's my secret. Those quiet open houses are good news for you. Winter is one of my favorite seasons to help buyers. This year the numbers back me up.

What the numbers say right now

Here's a snapshot of Lake Oswego from the Chicago Title market report dated November 15, 2026:

  • Median list price: $1,350,000
  • Homes for sale: 179
  • Median days on market: 84
  • Listings with a price cut: 49%

Almost half the homes for sale have dropped their price. The typical home sits close to three months. The report still calls this a slight seller's advantage, so don't expect a fire sale. Expect room to negotiate if you show up prepared.

Rates are moving your way

Freddie Mac's weekly survey put the average 30-year fixed rate at 7.11% on November 12, 2026. It was the fourth drop in a row and the lowest level in seven weeks. Rates are higher than a year ago, when the average sat at 6.24%. Even so, a small drop on a big loan shows up in your monthly payment.

Tam's tip: Don't chase the perfect rate. Chase the right home at the right price. Rates change every week. A seller who is ready to deal in December won't be waiting for you in April.

Why winter buyers win

Less competition. Fewer buyers tour in December. Fewer offers means less pressure to bid over list or skip your inspection.

Motivated sellers. A home on the market over the holidays usually has a reason to be there. A job move, a new home already bought or a year-end deadline. Motivation opens the door to credits, repairs or a better price.

You see the house on a bad day. Our rain shows you how the gutters drain, where water pools and how the crawlspace holds up. A sunny July showing hides all of it.

Easier scheduling. Inspectors, appraisers and title teams have more open spots this time of year. Your deadlines feel a lot less frantic.

How to make a winter offer count

Start with the price history. A home with a price cut and 80-plus days on market tells me the seller is listening. From there, we look at more than the price. Seller credits toward closing costs, a rate buydown, repairs or a flexible move-out date all have real value.

Keep your inspection. A slower market is no reason to skip it. It's your best chance to learn what you're buying and to ask for fixes before you sign.

Tam's tip: Book showings in daylight when you're able. The light disappears fast here in December. A 2 p.m. tour tells you far more about trees, slope and drainage than a 5 p.m. one.

Shopping lakefront or luxury?

Higher-end homes come with extra homework. Lake easements, dock rules and HOA dues all affect value. So does the 2025 court ruling allowing public entry to Oswego Lake from Lower Millennium Plaza Park, which the Lake Oswego Corporation has appealed. My Lakefront & Luxury page and Oswego Lake Access page walk you through it.

Moving here from out of town? Winter is a smart time to visit and see real daily life. Start with my Moving to Lake Oswego guide.

Your winter game plan

1. Book a call with me. We'll talk about what you want, what you need and your timeline. Then I'll connect you with lenders I trust.

2. Get pre-approved. A real pre-approval puts you in the strongest spot when a price drops and you want to move fast.

3. Pick your areas. Browse my neighborhood guides to see the parks, trails and shops near each one.

4. Watch the listings. Search homes and send me the ones you love. I'll dig into price history and days on market for each one.

5. Tour, then warm up. After a Saturday of showings, grab brunch at La Provence or a cozy dinner at Tucci's. House hunting is more fun with a plan for after.

Want the full step-by-step? My Buyer's Guide covers it all, from the first call to the keys.

The quiet season is your window

Spring brings more listings and more buyers. Winter brings fewer of both. If you're ready, this is your moment to buy with less pressure and more leverage.

Here's the truth. Handing someone keys in January, right as the new year starts, is one of my favorite feelings in this job. Let's make it yours. Book a buyer call and we'll build your winter plan together.
