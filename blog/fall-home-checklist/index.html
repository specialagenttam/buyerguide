Your Lake Oswego Fall Home Checklist: 9 Jobs Before the Rain Moves In

Fall here is gorgeous, then the rain shows up and stays until June. Here's how to get your home ready while the leaves are still pretty.

There's a week every October when Lake Oswego looks like a postcard. The maples go gold, the air smells like woodsmoke and everyone suddenly owns a cute scarf.

Then the rain shows up. And here in the Pacific Northwest, the rain doesn't visit. It moves in, unpacks and stays until June.

Your home handles the next eight months of wet a whole lot better if you give it a little love now. None of this is glamorous. All of it is cheaper than the call you'll make in January if you skip it.

1. Clean the gutters after the leaves drop, not before

Timing matters. Clean them in early October and you'll be back on the ladder by Thanksgiving. Wait until most of the leaves are down, then clear them out and run a hose through each downspout.

Clogged gutters overflow. Overflow soaks your siding, puddles against your foundation and finds its way into your crawl space. Water is patient. It always finds the weak spot.

Tam's tip: Walk your property in the first big rain with an umbrella and a cup of coffee. Watch where the water goes. Anything pooling against the house is a problem you just found for free.

2. Make sure downspouts send water away from the house

A downspout that dumps water right at the foundation is doing half its job. A simple extension or splash block pushes water a few feet out. Five minutes, a few dollars, real peace of mind.

3. Take a look at the crawl space

Most of us never go down there, and I get it. Grab a flashlight anyway. You're looking for standing water, a soggy or torn vapor barrier and anything that smells musty. Catching moisture now beats discovering it during a home inspection when you're trying to sell.

4. Blow out the sprinklers before the first hard freeze

Our freezes come quick and they don't send a warning. Water left in irrigation lines expands, cracks pipes and hands you a surprise repair bill in April. Book it now while the irrigation folks still have openings.

5. Disconnect hoses and cover outdoor faucets

A hose left attached traps water in the faucet, and that's how pipes inside the wall freeze and burst. Unscrew it, drain it and pop a foam cover over the spigot. They cost a few dollars at any hardware store.

6. Service the furnace and swap the filter

The first cold night is when every furnace in Portland kicks on, and it's when the HVAC companies' phones start ringing off the hook. A tune-up now catches small problems before they become a no-heat night in December. Change the filter while you're at it, and keep a spare on hand.

7. Test every smoke and carbon monoxide detector

Press the button on each one. Swap the batteries. With furnaces, fireplaces and space heaters all coming back to life, this one matters more in fall than any other season.

8. Get the chimney checked before your first fire

Few things beat a fire on a rainy Sunday. Few things are worse than a chimney fire. If you use a wood-burning fireplace, have it inspected and swept before the season starts.

9. Keep an eye on the moss

Moss loves it here as much as we do. On a roof it holds water against the shingles and shortens their life. On walkways and decks it turns into a slip hazard. Note where it's growing now and plan a treatment in spring.

Thinking of selling in spring? Moss, clogged gutters and a damp crawl space are three of the most common things buyers' inspectors flag around here. Handling them this fall means fewer surprises and a smoother negotiation later.

Bonus: plan for mud

Soggy yards are part of life here from now until spring. So are muddy footprints marching across your clean floors. A sturdy outdoor mat, an indoor mat and a boot tray by the door save you a lot of mopping.

If you're selling this season, take it one step further. Leave a basket of shoe covers by the front door and a sign asking showings to use them. Your floors stay clean between showings, and your home looks cared for every time a buyer walks in.

The real reason I care about this list

A well cared-for home is easier to live in and easier to sell. But more than that, your home is where your life happens. It deserves a little attention before the long gray stretch.
