Selling Your Lake Oswego Home: How to Win the First Two Weeks

Your strongest showings and offers usually come in the first two weeks. Here's how I help sellers get ready so those weeks count.

You only get one chance to be the new listing.

When a home hits the market, every buyer who's been waiting for something like it gets an alert at the same moment. They open the photos, scroll, decide. That first wave of attention is the strongest your home will ever see. Most of the best showings and strongest offers come in the first two weeks.

So everything we do before launch day is about one thing: making those two weeks count.

1. Start with your next chapter

Before we talk paint colors, let's talk about you. Where are you headed and when? Are you buying next, downsizing, relocating, combining households? Your next move shapes the listing date, how flexible you can be with buyers and which offers will actually fit your life.

This is also the part of selling that tugs at the heart. You might be leaving the home where your kids grew up, or where you hosted every holiday for twenty years. Selling is nerve-wracking for anyone, even when the move is a happy one.

I've taken courses in marriage and family counseling, and I lean on that training more than you'd think. Sometimes my job is pricing strategy. Sometimes it's sitting at your kitchen table while you decide which boxes go to storage and which memories go with you. I never rush that part. It matters.

2. Fix the little things buyers notice

Drippy faucets, sticky doors, burned-out bulbs, a cracked outlet cover. None of these are big. But buyers see three small things and start wondering what else got skipped. A handyman day before photos is one of the best investments a seller can make.

Fair warning: prep always takes longer than you think. I've had sellers tell me, "Oh, we'll be done in two weeks." Four months later, we listed. A beautiful listing, but four months! So give yourself more runway than seems necessary. Future you will be grateful.

Tam's tip: Walk through your home like a buyer would, starting at the curb. Phone in hand, write down everything that makes you wince. That list becomes our punch list.

3. Declutter and pack early

You're moving anyway, so get a head start. Clear the counters, thin out the closets and pack up the collections. Buyers need to picture their own life in your space, and they can't do that around your stuff. Bonus: moving day gets a lot easier.

4. Paint, clean and freshen

Neutral paint, a deep clean, clean windows and fresh carpet or refinished floors give you some of the best returns of any prep work. Photos love clean and bright. So do buyers.

5. Win the curb

The first photo buyers see is usually the front of your home. Mow, edge, fresh bark dust, a power-washed walkway, a sharp front door and a couple of pots of color. Around here, clear the moss off the walk and the roof too. It reads as "well cared for" before anyone steps inside.

6. Consider a pre-listing inspection

For older homes especially, knowing what a buyer's inspector will find gives you time to fix it or price for it. Fewer surprises means smoother negotiations, and smoother negotiations mean a calmer path to closing.

7. Price it right from day one

Here's the truth: overpricing is the most expensive mistake a seller can make. A home that sits starts to feel stale, and buyers begin asking what's wrong with it. Then the price drops, and the first-two-weeks advantage is gone.

I'll show you exactly what's selling nearby, what's sitting and why. We'll set a price that pulls buyers in instead of pushing them away.

8. Launch it like it matters

Professional photos, video, a floor plan, great listing copy and a real marketing plan. Your home deserves a launch, not a listing. Then I stay on top of every showing, every piece of feedback and every offer, and I'll update you every week so you always know where things stand.

You're not doing this alone

Selling a home is part spreadsheet and part goodbye. I'll handle the details, the vendors and the negotiating. You get to focus on the next chapter.
