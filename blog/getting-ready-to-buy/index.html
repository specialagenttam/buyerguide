Buying a Home in Lake Oswego: What I Tell Every Buyer Before the First Tour

Before you fall in love with a kitchen on Zillow, here's the conversation I have with every buyer. It saves heartbreak, money and a lot of second-guessing.

It always starts the same way. Someone sends me a listing at 11 p.m. with a string of heart-eye emojis and the words "Can we see this tomorrow?"

I love that text. I mean it. Falling for a home is the fun part. But the buyers who end up happiest are the ones who did a little work before they fell in love. So here's the conversation I have with every buyer before we ever step through a front door.

1. Start with a call with me

Before you talk numbers with anyone, let's talk about you. What you want, what you need, what's driving the move and what timeline feels right. Twenty minutes on the phone gives us a plan instead of a guessing game.

From there, I'll connect you with lenders I trust. Not the first name that pops up in an ad. People I've watched take great care of my clients when it counted.

2. Get pre-approved, not pre-qualified

These sound alike. They are not alike. A pre-qualification is a lender's best guess based on what you told them. A pre-approval means they actually reviewed your income, assets and credit.

When you write an offer, the seller wants to know you can close. A solid pre-approval tells them you're serious. In a multiple-offer situation, that can be the difference between "accepted" and "thanks for your interest."

3. Know your real monthly number

The loan payment is only part of the story. Add property taxes, homeowners insurance and any HOA dues. That total is the number to budget around, and it's the number we'll keep in mind when we tour.

Tam's tip: Pick the monthly payment you'd be comfortable with even in a tight month, not the most a lender says you can borrow. Those are often two very different numbers, and only one of them lets you sleep at night.

4. Save for more than the down payment

Closing costs, the inspection, the appraisal, the move itself and the first trip to the hardware store all add up. I'll walk you through what to expect so nothing catches you off guard on closing day.

5. Separate your must-haves from your nice-to-haves

Here's my favorite exercise. Make two lists. On the first, the things you can't change easily: number of bedrooms, location, commute, lot size, a single-level layout. On the second, everything else: paint colors, light fixtures, countertops, that wallpaper in the powder room.

The first list decides whether a home works. The second list is a weekend project. Buyers who know the difference move faster and regret less.

6. Think about how you actually live

Lake Oswego has a lot of personality packed into a small area. Some neighborhoods sit close to downtown shops and the lake. Some have trails out the back door. Some give you more room and quiet streets. I'll help you match homes and areas to your daily life: where you go, how you get there and what you love doing on a Saturday.

New to the area? My Lake Oswego guide is a great place to start.

7. Understand your buyer agreement

Before we tour, you'll sign an agreement with me. It spells out what I do for you and how I'm paid. I'll walk through every line and you can ask me anything. No fine print surprises. That's not how I work.

8. Know what happens after "accepted"

Getting your offer accepted feels like crossing the finish line. It's more like mile 20. Next comes the inspection, the appraisal, final loan approval and closing. I keep every deadline on track so you can focus on packing, picking paint colors and planning the housewarming.

Sometimes mile 20 gets messy. I'll never forget a purchase in Oregon City on a renovation loan. The septic system failed, and the deal fell apart. Then it fell apart again. And again. Three times we had to bring it back from the dead. It took nine months to close.

Those buyers could have walked away. So could I. We didn't. They love the home they ended up with, and when it came time to sell their next place, they called me. That's the kind of agent I want to be for you: the one who doesn't quit when it gets hard.

The part nobody puts on a checklist

Buying a home is a big, emotional, sometimes nerve-wracking thing. Feeling excited, scared and a little overwhelmed all in the same afternoon is completely normal. My job is to make the process clear, keep you calm and fight hard for you when it's time to negotiate.

I bought my first home as a single parent, and I still remember holding those keys and feeling like I'd done something huge. Because I had. So when you get your keys? That moment never gets old for me.

Ready to start? Book a buyer call and we'll build your plan together. Or start browsing homes and send me the ones that make your heart race.
